How the maths works

Absorbed energy is calculated, not randomised, from the standard hyperbolic-tangent transition model E(T) = LS + (US − LS)/2 · [1 + tanh((T − T₀)/C)]. LS is the lower-shelf (brittle cleavage) energy, US the upper-shelf (ductile shear) energy, T₀ the mid-energy DBTT and C the half-width of the transition zone. The values follow the known trend for normalised plain-carbon steels (Rinebolt & Harris): more carbon lowers the upper shelf and raises the transition temperature. Percent shear is taken as proportional to energy between the shelves, so the 50% FATT equals T₀. The 27 J line is the common acceptance criterion used in structural steel standards. Real results also depend on heat treatment, grain size and specimen orientation. Rise angle comes from E = mgR(cos β − cos α) with drop angle α = 150° on a ZwickRoell HIT450P pendulum impact tester (450 J maximum energy).
